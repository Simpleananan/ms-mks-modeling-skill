# Research design and model advancement

Load this reference for orientation, reconstruction, construction, candidate choice, repair, convergence, or model-facing writing. Do not expose it as a checklist.

## Model quality follows the research question

A useful model makes its claim-bearing links identifiable:

`problem -> formal objects -> endogenous response -> equilibrium/solution -> mechanism or other contribution object -> claim boundary`.

Evaluate the model relative to what the paper intends to contribute:

- **Research and institutional fit:** the modeled actors, control rights, timing, information, actions, and constraints must carry the stated phenomenon. Purposeful abstraction is acceptable; a factual mismatch matters when it changes the research object or strategic feedback.
- **Construct specificity:** a construct-specific claim needs a formal feature that makes the construct consequential. A generic reduced form is valid when the claim is deliberately general and the application mapping is credible. Renaming a generic parameter is a warning, not an automatic veto.
- **Mechanism and informativeness:** identify the endogenous dependency producing the result. Opposing effects, thresholds, or surprising signs are not required. A direct effect can contribute through a new institution, equilibrium object, formal solution, boundary, or welfare implication, but should not be advertised as a new strategic mechanism without such feedback.
- **Assumption load:** assess an assumption by its role, conclusion load, and result dependency, not by calling it strong or weak. A desired result written directly into an assumption carries high risk.
- **Solution quality:** closed form is optional. Implicit characterizations, fixed points, numerical equilibria, computational methods, and simulations are acceptable when existence or convergence, domain, sensitivity, interpretation, and claim scope are adequate.
- **Contribution fit:** possible deltas include a research question, institution, primitive, strategic response, mechanism, solution method, prediction, welfare implication, or boundary. Mechanism novelty is not the only legitimate contribution.

Do not combine these dimensions into a universal score. Rank concerns by whether they can change the research question, game, mechanism, equilibrium, main claim, contribution, or welfare conclusion. Move minor realism, notation, and exposition issues below claim-bearing risks.

## Orientation from incomplete input

When the user supplies only a phenomenon, institution, target paper, case, broad direction, or uncertainty:

1. Recover facts already supplied.
2. Identify the institution, strategic actors, control and information allocation, and possible strategic tension.
3. State a provisional research object and the endogenous decision that could carry it.
4. Identify the current bottleneck: institutional knowledge, research-question ambiguity, missing strategic interaction, literature collision, tractability, or a decisive formal test.
5. Advance one coherent path when it already works. Generate multiple non-equivalent paths only when a live branch can change the research object, game, mechanism, contribution, or tractability.
6. Retrieve evidence when it can eliminate, sharpen, or compare those live paths.
7. Ask one batched question only if surviving paths remain structurally different and the user holds the missing information or research preference.

Sparse-idea generation is experimental. Present candidate questions and minimum formalizations as provisional rather than declaring a final paper idea.

## Target-paper reconstruction

Before explaining, evaluating, or extending a target paper, recover its research question, institution, applicable formal objects, endogenous dependencies, solution concept, benchmark or identifying comparison, main mechanism or contribution object, load-bearing assumptions, and claim boundary. For a game, this normally activates players, timing, information, actions, payoffs, beliefs, deviations, and equilibrium. For optimization, dynamic decision, information design, or another analytical form, reconstruct the relevant primitives, states, controls, objective, constraints, transition/commitment structure, and solution. Treat the main article and linked appendix as one model-bearing set whenever the appendix contains proofs, assumptions, omitted cases, extensions, or counterexamples that can change the requested understanding; an abstract cannot establish exact assumptions or proof completeness.

Match depth to the user's objective. A paper-understanding request needs faithful reconstruction and the load-bearing solution dependency, not an unsolicited exhaustive reviewer report. After reconstruction, make one proportionate attempt to falsify or narrow a load-bearing claim when the appendix, proof, domain, or internal consistency makes that decision-relevant. Report a supported concern even for a published paper, but distinguish a baseline theorem failure from a robustness limitation, claim-scope overstatement, exposition gap, likely typo, or unresolved extraction issue. Activate redesign or novelty screening only when requested or when an inconsistency prevents faithful understanding.

Separate:

- **reconstructed design logic** supported by the final paper's structure; and
- **historical model genesis**, which requires versions, process materials, or explicit author statements.

When chronology evidence is absent, state once that the explanation reconstructs design logic rather than the authors' actual sequence of thought.

## Build and solve

For a new or completed model:

1. Define the research object and intended claim domain.
2. Map the institution before selecting convenient equations.
3. Specify timing, information, actions, and commitment together; each strategy must be feasible at its information set.
4. Define payoffs, constraints, outside options, transfers, and accounting identities as applicable.
5. Select the weakest solution concept that answers the research question.
6. Derive how later-stage responses, beliefs, continuation values, or feasible correspondences enter earlier decisions.
7. Identify which primitive or endogenous response carries each important result.
8. Mark unresolved formal debt and preserve branches only when they define genuinely different games.

For “how is the model solved?”, explain both solution order and dependency. Name the object produced at the later stage and how it changes the earlier objective, constraint, belief, or equilibrium condition. Expand full FOCs, SOCs, posteriors, integrals, or threshold algebra only when requested or necessary to audit the conclusion.

## Literature streams and recomposition

Separate retrieval into formal streams when a question draws on distinct traditions. Use each stream to recover relevant primitives and strategic responses. Combine streams in one baseline only when they share a consequential strategic seam: the same endogenous decision, state, signal, control variable, payoff dependency, best-response effect, or equilibrium feedback. Topical overlap alone supports an extension, separate branch, or deletion—not baseline combination.

## Candidate generation and selection

Generate candidates only when the current path fails or a live design choice can change the research object, institution, mechanism, timing/information/actions, equilibrium, contribution, or tractability. Do not invent alternatives merely to satisfy a comparison format.

For live candidates, compare only decision-relevant deltas:

- research question and institutional fit;
- primitive and strategic-response changes;
- equilibrium and claim changes;
- assumption load and risk of result engineering;
- mechanism identifiability and solution burden;
- nearest-neighbor collision;
- whether a smaller model answers the same question;
- whether the candidate silently changes the research object.

Use three calibrated outcomes:

- **Select** when one candidate has a concrete advantage and no unresolved issue could plausibly reverse it.
- **Conditional select** when ranking depends on the research objective, institutional fidelity, contribution object, or tractability constraint; state the flip condition.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** when formal feasibility, objective, institution, or nearest-neighbor evidence can still change the winner.

Seek a cheap decisive test when one exists: a limiting case, sign decomposition, active constraint, deviation, nonempty-domain condition, or literature collision. Do not force a binary test when several independent uncertainties remain.

## Repair and redesign

After diagnosing a consequential obstacle:

1. Trace it to the primitive, strategic response, feasibility condition, or equilibrium dependency that produces it.
2. Decide whether the research intuition is incomplete or the current model omits/misrepresents an intended institutional force.
3. Construct only causally distinct responses: repair the link, redesign the relevant game element, narrow the claim, or accept the result and revise the theory.
4. Use the smallest repair only when it preserves the research object and mechanism. Redesign primitives, timing, information, actions, market structure, or solution concept when the current game is the wrong formal object.
5. Retrieve a precedent or institutional fact only when it can change the available repairs.
6. Test the cheapest decisive implication before fully solving each candidate.
7. Re-solve every dependency affected by the selected change; prose alone is not a repair.

Do not manufacture a mechanism to recover a preferred sign. If no credible repair survives, treat that negative conclusion as research information.

## Convergence and extensions

Freeze the baseline provisionally when it answers the research question, defines a sufficiently closed game, supports an identifiable mechanism or other clearly stated contribution object, has no unresolved structural veto, and has enough nearest-neighbor evidence for the current decision. Optional realism, another imaginable candidate, or an extension does not justify delaying solution work.

Use extensions for a specific job: mechanism necessity, boundary expansion, endogenization, alternative explanation, robustness, or a deliberately new research object. Label the last category honestly rather than presenting it as routine robustness.

## Working Model Draft

For cross-session work, keep the active model synchronized after changes to primitives, timing, information, actions, payoffs, solution concept, mechanism, or claim boundary. Preserve the current model rather than a transcript. Retain a rejected branch only as a short guard with the reason and a condition for reopening it.

**Draft follows model; draft never determines model.** Revise the story when formal results change. Do not alter the model to preserve attractive prose or retroactively attribute a new repair rationale to the author or earlier model.
